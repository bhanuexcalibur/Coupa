Part 01 The Angle

Coupa's real PMM challenge isn't messaging.
It's translation.

Coupa has one of the most defensible data moats in enterprise software. The question is whether it's being communicated in a way that procurement leaders, CFOs, and supply chain VPs actually feel — not just understand.

What Coupa actually has

The community data advantage is real, but underplayed

$8 trillion in spend data across 10M+ buyers and suppliers. Coupa's community intelligence isn't just a feature — it's the reason its agentic AI recommendations carry actual weight. When Coupa tells a CPO that a supplier is high-risk, it's informed by anonymised signals from thousands of other companies running comparable spend. No other platform has that. But right now, the way this gets communicated often sounds like a feature spec. It needs to sound like an insurance policy.

The positioning gap

Everyone says "AI." Not everyone means it the same way.

Post-Thoma Bravo, Coupa is pushing hard on agentic AI, autonomous spend management, and the "living trade network" vision. Inspiring internally. But buyers are drowning in AI claims from every vendor in their stack. The PMM job here is to make Coupa's AI claim specific, verifiable, and tied to a business outcome — not just a headline. "Community-generated AI" is a great differentiator, but it needs a proof point the CFO can quote in a board meeting.

The Inspire 2025 signal

Coupa is repositioning from spend tool to trade network. Most of the market hasn't caught up.

Leagh Turner's "we're not really a software company, we're a community" framing from Inspire 2025 is the clearest articulation of what Coupa is actually building. The Cirtuo acquisition, the Scoutbee acquisition for supplier intelligence, the Salesforce AppExchange integration — these aren't product updates. They're positioning moves. Strategic programs PMM exists to connect these dots for the market before analysts do it for them.

What I'd bring

Cross-functional coordination for programs that actually land

Strategic Programs isn't just campaign work. It's launch coordination, event content, cross-team enablement, and the behind-the-scenes muscle that makes PMM output usable rather than decorative. I've worked across content, positioning, competitive, and campaign layers — which means I can take a product announcement from brief to field-ready collateral without needing six hand-offs to get there.

One thing I noticed: Coupa's homepage currently leads with "Make Margins Multiply" — which is a strong CFO hook. But the path from that headline to a concrete product story for a procurement director or a supply chain VP is longer than it needs to be. That's the kind of gap strategic programs should close.
Part 02 Messaging Audit

Three products. Three messaging gaps.

This is a quick audit of how Coupa currently positions three of its flagship areas — and where the language could do more work. Not a takedown: a set of things I'd want to pressure-test if I were briefing the team on what to fix first.

What it currently sounds like
  • "Community-generated AI" as a label without a mechanism — buyers don't know what it means in practice
  • AI framed as predictions and prescriptions — still sounds like a dashboard feature
  • No clear answer to the question: "Why is Coupa's AI better than my ERP vendor's AI?"
What it could sound like
  • Lead with the data source: $8T of anonymised spend, 10M buyers and suppliers — that's what trains the model
  • Make the recommendation tangible: "Coupa flagged a supplier risk before three of our top accounts felt it" is the story
  • The counter-positioning to ERP AI: Coupa's model is trained on spend, not support tickets
Proposed reframe — elevator version

"Most AI tools learn from your data. Coupa's AI learns from $8 trillion in spend decisions made by thousands of companies before you. When it tells you a supplier is about to be a problem, it's probably already been a problem somewhere else."

Why this works

It leads with the moat (community data), delivers the proof (other companies have already stress-tested this), and ties back to the outcome a CPO cares about (supply risk, not software features). No technical jargon. No competitor callout needed.

What it currently sounds like
  • Supply chain messaging leans heavily on "resilience" and "visibility" — words every vendor uses
  • The LLamasoft heritage (network design, demand modeling) isn't surfaced in a way that differentiates from point solutions
  • No clear before/after for what the supply chain team's day actually changes
What it could sound like
  • Lead with the problem that's actually expensive: discovering a sourcing risk in the boardroom instead of in the model
  • The design-to-pay angle is the real differentiator — most competitors own one end but not both
  • Concrete use case: "We ran 40 network scenarios before the tariff decision. We picked the one that held."
Proposed reframe — elevator version

"Supply chain tools tell you what happened. Coupa's design-to-pay platform helps you model what's about to happen — before you've committed the spend. It's the difference between reacting to a disruption and pricing it in before it lands."

Why this works

The supply chain VP's fear isn't a bad dashboard. It's a board meeting where someone asks why the disruption wasn't caught earlier. This reframe speaks to that fear and positions Coupa's end-to-end model as the answer — not just another visibility layer.

What it currently sounds like
  • Contract Intelligence is positioned as a legal/compliance tool — which buries the commercial value
  • "AI-powered clause recommendations" sounds like a legal word processor, not a spend control layer
  • No clear ICP: this could be legal, procurement, or finance — and right now it's trying to speak to all three at once
What it could sound like
  • Frame it from procurement's side: every poorly-worded contract is a future spend leak waiting to happen
  • The Agentforce integration (Salesforce AppExchange) is a big deal — it means contracts live in the workflow, not in a silo
  • Lead with the moment it pays off: "The audit that didn't turn into a crisis"
Proposed reframe — elevator version

"Every procurement contract is a promise about future spend. Coupa Contract Intelligence makes sure the promise you signed is the one you're actually keeping — and flags the gaps before your auditors do."

Why this works

It brings contracts back into the spend conversation (where Coupa's positioning is strongest), gives the procurement buyer a personal stake in the outcome, and sets up the risk framing that makes a CFO pay attention. Legal sells fewer seats than compliance risk does.

Part 04 Launch Brief

Coupa Navi: A worked launch brief

Coupa Navi launched in September 2024 as a generative AI agent for real-time business query support. Here's the launch brief I'd have written. The structure is the same one I'd bring to any product launch at Coupa — regardless of the specific product or program.

🎯 Launch Objective

  • Primary: Position Coupa Navi as the "co-pilot for procurement" — not a chatbot, but an agent that already knows your spend context
  • Secondary: Differentiate from generic AI assistants by leading with the community data advantage
  • Business metric: Drive Navi adoption among existing customers within 90 days of GA; reduce support ticket volume as a proxy metric

👥 Target Audiences

  • Tier 1: Current Coupa customers (VP Procurement, CPO) — activation, not acquisition
  • Tier 2: Prospects in active pipeline — Navi as a deal-accelerating proof point
  • Tier 3: Analysts and press — category definition moment ("autonomous procurement is real, and Coupa shipped it")

✍️ Key Messages by Audience

  • CPO: "Your team is spending time on questions your data already knows the answer to."
  • CFO: "Faster, more accurate answers from procurement without more headcount."
  • IT / Ops: "No integration project. It runs on your existing Coupa data."

📦 Deliverables Checklist

  • One-pager for sales (not a data sheet — a conversation starter)
  • Objection-handling guide: "How is this different from asking my ERP?"
  • Customer launch email sequence: 2-touch, outcome-led
  • Event session framing for Inspire: demo-first, results-led
  • Competitive snapshot: vs. SAP Joule, vs. Oracle AI

90-Day Launch Calendar

Days 1–14
Internal readiness

Sales enablement session, battlecard finalized, FAQ doc shipped to CS team. Internal Slack launch moment with Navi demo clip — make the team feel it before external launch.

Days 15–30
Customer-first launch

Email to existing customers — outcome-led, no feature list. Early access invite for power users. Customer success team briefed to capture first use-case stories within 30 days.

Days 31–60
Market activation

Press and analyst briefings. Blog post from a customer, not from Coupa. LinkedIn campaign anchored to one specific use case (supplier risk query response time). Event integration at regional summits.

Days 61–90
Proof and momentum

First customer story published. Win/loss review with sales to see where Navi is showing up in deals. Retro with PMM team: what messaging landed, what didn't, what to carry into Inspire prep.
